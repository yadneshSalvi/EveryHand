One little change.
Follow it through.

Every Hand turns a workshop plan into individual equipment turns. This map follows a real choice: keep the equipment and add time, or keep the finish time and add a place. Preview a named learner’s path, then apply the verified schedule to the room and export. It is a reference route; no human code walkthrough is claimed.

A three-stop route

01

Preview a real choice

src/ChoiceWorkspace.tsx · ChoiceWorkspace

The comparison offers actual time/equipment options. Constraint locks filter what can change; named before/after paths show the people affected. Preview leaves the workshop unchanged.

02

Verify the change

src/choices.ts · generateChoices / applyChoice

The engine calls the scheduler with unchanged full durations and protected intervals. Applying checks source revision, current locks and actual slot evidence. Single-activity minima are exact; multi-activity options are verified, bounded searches.

03

Inspect the same evidence

src/App.tsx · applyAlternative

The selected plan and result commit together, with undo. src/Room.tsx · Room reads the real slots. The roster and src/export.ts · toCSV read those same slots, so the visual count and downloaded evidence agree.

A practice to reuse

Write a numerical acceptance example before building the interface. Here, 28 × 4 learners/minutes with three microscopes in thirty minutes must yield 21 complete turns. That fixture in src/solver.test.ts catches fractional-person arithmetic. src/choices.test.ts separately checks the 40-minute baseline choice and the 36-minute recovery with eight completed turns preserved. On another project, pair one observable requirement with a small test that would catch a plausible mistake.

What this map doesn't prove

These are planned opportunities, not observed participation. Multi-activity scheduling is deterministic but heuristic. Actual classroom durations, human final review and the narrated demo remain separate work.
